In November 2022, I was about to take one of my dream trips.

And so, one November evening, I found myself on a plane heading to Havana. I arrived at night, so my first day-night consisted of being picked up from the airport and getting some sleep at the place I had booked.

No, I didn’t travel all the way to the other side of the world alone. I’m not that brave, but I’ll present it as if I did.

## **Day 1**

My first full day started with exploring the city in **Old Havana**.

With no internet access yet, I found my way to the heart of Havana using an offline map. A different world, I would say. Colonial buildings, some more run-down and poorly maintained, with voices, laughter and music constantly spilling out of them, while others stood proudly through time, more iconic than ever. Cobblestone streets and large historic squares unfolded right in front of me. You get mixed feelings when visiting places like these. It feels as if time has stopped in another era, another decade. Another characteristic of this country is its vintage cars. Coming straight from other decades, they somehow find their way into the present and become the country's trademark.

First stop: coffee and breakfast. By chance, I stumbled upon a lovely café called **El Café**, which became my go-to spot every day. The options are generally simpler than what I’m used to in Europe, but it was a good choice.

![](/images/uploads/img_7633.jpeg)

As I continued exploring, I found myself admiring some of the city’s most iconic buildings, including **Cuba’s National Capitol Building**. Unfortunately, I didn’t get the chance to go inside, but if you find yourself there and have the opportunity, it’s worth stepping in to admire some of its highlights, such as the Statue of the Republic (La Estatua de la República) and Kilometer Zero (Kilómetro Cero).

<figure class="collage" data-layout="row">
<img src="/images/uploads/img_7634.jpeg" alt="">
<img src="/images/uploads/img_7650.jpeg" alt="">

Although I didn’t manage to get inside the Capitol, I was luckier with the **Grand Theatre of Havana (Gran Teatro de La Habana Alicia Alonso)**. Not all areas were accessible due to renovations, but it was still worth taking a look inside this impressive building along Paseo del Prado. If I remember correctly, Ι had to buy a ticket at the entrance, and a guide was available. It’s generally not very crowded, so I was inside in no time. The views from inside were just as magical.

Next, a stop along Havana’s famous seaside promenade, **El Malecón**, was exactly what I needed. A little break to sit back, take it all in, and watch the city unfold in front of me. And somewhere along the way, it finally hit me. I was actually in Cuba.

![](/images/uploads/img_4628.jpg)

## **Day 2**

Day two started with a road trip. I had already booked a taxi, but it was nothing like the kind of taxis you’d expect or are used to seeing in other countries. It was an antique... I’m not great with car brands, but it was definitely vintage, let’s put it that way. It looked like something straight out of an old black-and-white Greek movie.

When the taxi arrived to pick me up, we set off for **Varadero**. It’s a more touristy destination, I’d say, with unbelievably blue waters, about two hours away from Havana. What I enjoyed most was swimming in the sea and simply relaxing. There isn’t a huge amount to do in the area apart from the resorts, so you might get a little bored after a few hours, but it’s definitely worth a visit.

Back in Havana, I ended the day with dinner at **Los Mercaderes**, if I remember correctly. I have to admit, I really struggled when it came to food. Let’s be honest, Cuban cuisine isn’t exactly known for being one of the most impressive out there. That said, there are some restaurants with really good food, such as Los Mercaderes and **La Guarida**, where I had dinner on another evening. Both are also set in incredibly beautiful buildings.

## **Day 3 & 4**

Day three started with another trip, this time with an overnight stay. My destination? **Trinidad**. What a beautiful place! I decided to keep my accommodation in Havana and spend one night in Trinidad with just a small backpack. It made the whole trip much easier.

Just like the day before, the taxi I had booked picked me up early in the morning. The most interesting part of this roughly five-hour journey was my fellow travellers. Two Italians, an American solo traveller, and “I” ended up meeting on the other side of the world, all heading to the same destination. I may not remember their names today, but meeting each other, exchanging stories and sharing moments as strangers is what fascinates me about travelling and makes me appreciate every single moment of it.

After a few hours on the road and a couple of stops, we finally arrived, and I was absolutely blown away by the hotel. It looked like something straight out of a fairytale. Magical! The houses have a unique architectural style that draws you in, even if they’re nothing like the modern buildings we’re used to. It’s as if they’ve been transported from another century, and that’s exactly where their charm lies.

## **Day 5**

Here I am, back in Havana, wandering around the city once again.

I found myself around **Plaza de la Catedral**, admiring the architecture of **Havana Cathedral**, both inside and out. Although I’m not a religious person, Catholic churches have a special kind of charm for me. I love taking my time to look around and admire all the little details. 

<figure class="collage" data-layout="row">
<img src="/images/uploads/img_7886.jpeg" alt="">
<img src="/images/uploads/img_7911.jpeg" alt="">
</figure>

My next stop was the **City Museum (Museo de la Ciudad)**, a little journey back to the 19th century, with old horse-drawn carriages and other pieces of Cuban history.

![](/images/uploads/img_4728.jpg)

Since the day was all about exploring the area’s history and iconic landmarks, a visit to **Che Guevara’s** former home-turned-museum had to be part of the plan, too. I took the ferry from Muelle de Luz, in Old Havana, heading towards Casablanca. The journey takes around 10 minutes.

My first stop was the **Christ of Havana statue (Cristo de La Habana)**. You’ll find one of these statues almost everywhere! This one isn’t quite as iconic or as massive as the one in Brazil, but the views from up there are beautiful.

![](/images/uploads/img_4750.jpg)

Next, I visited **Centro Cultural Casa del Che (La Cabaña de Che Guevara)**, where you pay a small entrance fee to visit the museum. It’s a relatively small place, I’d say, but one with a story of its own.

<figure class="collage" data-layout="feature">

Just a few steps away, on the same side of the city, you’ll find the **Fortress of San Carlos de la Cabaña (Fortaleza de San Carlos de la Cabaña)**, which offers incredible views across the bay towards Havana.

![](/images/uploads/img_4774.jpg)

That evening, I was supposed to visit **Fábrica de Arte Cubano**, but guess what… I fell asleep! So, I guess I’ll have to leave that one for another time. Still, I’ve heard nothing but good things about the place. From what people have told me, it feels like stepping into a completely different side of Cuba. Don’t make the same mistake I did! If you ever find yourself in Havana, make sure you go!

## **Day 6**

My last day, and another taxi ride on the agenda. Surprised?

The day included horseback riding and a visit to a farm to see how the famous Cuban cigars are made. Along the way, our driver managed to squeeze in a quick stop at **Fusterlandia**, an entire neighbourhood transformed into a surreal, open-air work of art. It’s open to the public and accessible to everyone. Another little hidden gem in this city.

Our last stop before heading back was the **Mural de la Prehistoria**. Another work of art, this time a giant outdoor painting on the side of a hill, depicting the evolution of life in the region.

![](/images/uploads/img_4815.jpg)

It was a full day, and one that brought me closer to nature than ever.

And that was my experience in Cuba.

Of course, there were also aspects of the country that made me reflect on the injustices in the world, but I won’t get into that here.

For now, I’ll leave you with the beautiful side of this place.

Cuba is a dream!
