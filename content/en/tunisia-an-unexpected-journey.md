I have a thing for visiting warm destinations just before winter kicks in. A little escape from the gloomy Dutch weather.

Since my plans for another trip had fallen through, I basically invited myself along on a trip my friend Emmy had already planned. I just couldn't stay put. I would have gone crazy!

A destination I had never even considered visiting, which eventually turned out to be an amazing place and one I would definitely recommend adding to your travel list.

The trip started in November 2023, once again around my birthday. I guess I get the urge to escape every year as I get a little older. A train, a flight, and voilà! We made it to Tunis, Tunisia.

We took a taxi and got to our hotel easily and pretty quickly. An incredible building with amazing architecture. Absolutely beautiful! What was it called? I honestly can't remember...

After checking in, changing clothes, and taking a little breather, we headed out to explore. We wandered around the area, making our way towards the **Medina of Tunis**.

Most of the shops and stores were already closed by then, as it was getting dark. We found some random fast-food place and decided to grab a bite there. I don't think I've ever eaten that cheaply in my life!

## **The first full day**

After unsuccessfully trying to get some sleep all night (because snoring and my friend seem to come as a package deal), sorry Emmy, we finally got up, got ready, and hit the streets again, after having breakfast at the hotel. If you've read my Brazil posts, you'll already know about our walking tours. Well, here's another one to add to the list!

We were a small group of just three people, including our guide. We spent hours walking around, visiting the city's main attractions and learning not only about its history but also about the local culture and the people who live there. As a predominantly Muslim and Arab country, Tunisia was quite different. And since we were two women travelling on our own, we were a little more mindful of what we wore and generally tried to adapt or, better yet, blend in with our surroundings, showing respect for the place and the people.

It's interesting to learn about all the different religious communities that coexist in a predominantly Muslim society. What impressed us the most, though, was that even though we mostly saw men out on the streets, in restaurants and cafés, the respect we received and the sense of safety we felt were truly remarkable. And that's exactly what fascinates me about visiting places I'm not familiar with. You arrive with a certain perspective, only to find some of your assumptions being turned upside down.

Starting at **Place de l'Indépendance**, we continued through the narrow streets of the **Medina**, squeezed our way through **Souk Al Attarine**, passed by **Victoria Square**, discovered a few different viewpoints, and eventually found ourselves walking through the central market, surrounded by all kinds of smells. Some pleasant, others not so much, especially since you can find pretty much everything there in its raw form. These were just some of the things we did and saw during the tour. Basically, we had already covered all the city's must-visit spots on our very first day, and quite effortlessly, too!

Our guide also bought us some local sweets to try. They actually reminded me quite a bit of the traditional syrup-soaked desserts we have back in Greece. Turns out, our cuisines have quite a few things in common. Towards the end of the tour, he took us to a lovely local café tucked away among the busy market streets.

We spent the rest of the day heading back to **Souk Al Attarine**, this time to do some bargaining for souvenirs. I have to admit, they were quite expensive considering the country's prices. What really surprised us, though, was how easily the sellers figured out we were Greek and immediately started speaking to us in Greek! For dinner, we chose **Fondouk El Attarine**, a restaurant serving Mediterranean and local cuisine. It had an outdoor seating area and, to be honest, quite a lot of people. Still, it turned out to be a very good choice. And just like that, we headed back to the hotel to relax for a while, enjoying the view and gazing at the stars from our little balcony...

## **The second full day**

If there's one thing you've probably figured out by now, it's that I don't really sit still when I travel. I always try to squeeze in as much as possible during the days I have available. So, naturally, another little trip was added to this trip!

And that's how we met Youyou. An incredible guy, more or less our age, who made our journey and the days we spent there truly unforgettable. He picked us up from the hotel, and we set off towards our first destination, the **Tozeur** region. It was supposed to be a journey of around six hours, but it ended up taking quite a bit longer as we made several stops along the way.

The first one was somewhere halfway through the drive, where our driver insisted on treating us to some traditional tea with almonds. Definitely not my favourite, as in my mind tea equals being sick, and I'm not particularly fond of nuts in general either. Still, I appreciated the gesture!

Our next official stop was the city of **Kairouan** and the **Great Mosque of Kairouan (Mosque of Uqba)**. It was my first time visiting a mosque, and it was really interesting to see a place of worship up close and learn about the way people pray in a religion so different from our "own".

![](/images/uploads/img_4320.jpeg)

After taking the "obligatory" photos in the courtyard, we continued to our next stop, the **Chebika Oasis**. And that's when I finally understood the true meaning of the word "oasis". It's hard to capture in a single photo what my eyes got to see. A place of absolute peace and tranquillity. One of those landscapes that make you stop for a moment and simply take it all in. Perhaps the most beautiful scenery I had ever seen up to that point.

## **The third full day**

The next day, we packed our bags again, checked out of the hotel, and headed towards the **Sahara Desert**, with more photos and some camel-riding activities on the agenda. I had already ridden a camel in Jordan, but I'll save that story for another post. For Emmy, though, it was her first time, and she was pretty excited!

Next stop... **Mos Espa!** All the real Star Wars fans know what I'm talking about. Me? Well, I'm not one of them, so I can't say I was particularly impressed. Sorry, fans!

And just like that, we found ourselves in the middle of nowhere, with a replica of the Titanic and only about 150 kilometres away from the Algerian border. Along the way, Youyou made another stop to show us some small mountains of salt. Tunisia has a long history of salt extraction, so it's quite common to come across these formations, as well as the famous desert roses.

## **The fourth full day**

Another day on the road, this time heading towards **Matmata**. A traditional Berber village in southern Tunisia, mainly known for its underground homes. The locals, as welcoming as ever, invited us into their homes to have a look inside these famous dwellings. A truly different experience!

It was also the day we headed back to Tunis, which meant quite a few hours on the road. I think this is probably a good moment to do a little recap of our experience so far.

Despite all the hours spent driving, the long distances and the many different landscapes we got to see, what really made this experience even better was our driver. Youyou took the time to show us as much as possible in those three days, took countless photos of us, looked after us and, of course, gave us the chance to share a little bit of our cultures with each other. If you ever decide to do this trip, make sure to ask for Youyou. I'll happily give you his number in a heartbeat!

Another thing that turned out to be an experience in itself was all those little stops along the way. The kind of stops you might not necessarily mention when talking about a trip because they're not exactly the highlights, but they're such a big part of the whole experience.

Stopping in the middle of nowhere to grab whatever "questionable" food we could find, surrounded mostly by men, and yet feeling completely safe. The respect we received, even though we stood out as tourists from a mile away. The spicy food, the laughter, even when we couldn't understand a word the people around us were saying... All of these little moments are what make a travel experience even more unique.

## **The fifth full day**

We were now on our fifth hotel (if I'm not mistaken) of this trip, and the last one! Our return to Tunis came with a little unexpected twist, as the hotel we had booked had been cancelled, and we only realised it when we got back to the city. Luckily, we managed to find an alternative quite easily and pretty quickly.

Our first stop was the **Carthage Archaeological Site**, including the Roman amphitheatre and the **Antonine Baths**. Such an impressive place, with so many remains of ancient times still standing after all these centuries, helping your imagination bring the past back to life and picture what things must have looked like back then.

After exploring the site, we headed to **Sidi Bou Said**. A place where, the moment you set foot in it, you feel as if you've landed on a Greek island. A Tunisian village with a very Mediterranean island vibe.

We wandered through the cobbled, blue-and-white streets, surrounded by other tourists, with endless blue views all around us. We enjoyed our coffee overlooking the sea, our eyes getting lost in the endless shades of blue. A view that completely captivated us and took us back to Greece for a little while.

In the evening, we returned to Tunis, with only a few hours left before heading back to our everyday lives.

A destination I would wholeheartedly recommend, even if you've never considered visiting it before. It is more than worth it!
